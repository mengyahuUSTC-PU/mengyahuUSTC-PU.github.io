### [OpenAI explains what actually caused the Codex file-deletion bug](https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2077630111499882637)

Thibault Sottiaux laid out the root cause publicly: running with full access outside sandbox mode, the model tried to override the `$HOME` environment variable to set up a temporary directory, and — in what Sottiaux called an honest mistake — deleted the actual `$HOME` directory instead. This wasn't a case of the model "going rogue" — it's a plainer engineering failure. Once an agent has unsandboxed write access to your home directory, a single mistaken command is all it takes. Whether sandboxing should be on by default, and how much convenience developers are willing to trade away to keep it on, is a tradeoff every agentic coding tool now has to face. (See also: [Simon Willison's note](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/16/bad-codex-bug/).)

### [xAI open-sources grok CLI — after a privacy backlash, not before](https://github.com/xai-org/grok-build)

The grok-build CLI was previously found to upload a user's entire working directory without clear disclosure, SSH keys and password vaults included, which triggered real backlash. xAI has now open-sourced it. Put next to the Codex incident, the pattern is structural, not a one-off: agentic tools default to file-access scopes that are far too broad, and "trust the local directory by default" is itself the flawed assumption. Open-sourcing the code doesn't fix the permission model, but it does make external auditing possible. (See also: [Simon Willison's note](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/15/grok-build/).)

### [Kimi K3 and Inkling push open weights toward the 3T-parameter tier](https://www.kimi.com/blog/kimi-k3)

Moonshot AI shipped Kimi K3, a 2.8T-parameter model it's calling the first "open 3T-class" release, with weights promised by July 27. Around the same time, Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab put out its first open-weight model, [Inkling](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/introducing-inkling/) — a 975B MoE with 41B active parameters. Taken together, the open camp's competitive axis has shifted from "will you open-source" to "how close can your open model's parameter count get to the closed frontier" — a real stress test on training and inference cost. Whether the benchmarks back up the parameter-count narrative is the thing to watch next. (See also: Simon Willison on [Kimi K3](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/16/kimi-k3/) and [Inkling](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/16/inkling/).)

### [Anthropic makes Fable 5 permanent in Premium tiers](https://x.com/claudeai/status/2078302417100394737)

Starting July 20, Fable 5 becomes a standing model in Max/Team Premium subscriptions. The move itself isn't surprising, but the timing — right on the heels of competitive pressure from GPT-5.6 Sol — suggests release cadence is increasingly being set by competitors' shipping schedules rather than an independent roadmap. (See also: [Simon Willison's note](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/18/claude-make-fable-5-permanent/).)

### [GPT-5.6 allegedly closed a 30-year gap in convex optimization — don't buy it yet](https://old.reddit.com/r/math/comments/1uxj3cy/after_openais_cdc_proof_announcement_gpt56_used_a/)

A popular Reddit thread (also [discussed on Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48957779)) claims a single prompt to GPT-5.6 helped fill a proof gap that had stood open in convex optimization for 30 years, but the comment section is already picking at the rigor of the proof. This kind of "AI solves a decades-old math problem" story has been showing up on a near-monthly cadence, and the arc is familiar: a splashy headline first, followed by the math community slowly finding the holes. Until peer review actually confirms it, this belongs in the "worth watching" pile, not the "fact" pile.

### [Global Index on Responsible AI 2026 report released](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14782v1)

GIRAI 2026 scores countries across five dimensions on how well their responsible-AI commitments actually get implemented. For anyone working in Responsible AI, cross-country quantitative comparisons like this are rare enough to be worth a closer look once the specific rankings and methodology are available.

**One line for today**: two agentic-tool security incidents in a single week say the real constraint on these tools isn't model capability — it's permission design. Whoever ships "least privilege" as the default first is the one who avoids the next headline.
