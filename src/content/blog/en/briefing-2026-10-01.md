### [OpenAI fires three safety researchers, days after shelving GPT-6.1](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/01/openai-cuts-ties-with-three-safety-researchers-wsj-reports/)

Per the Wall Street Journal (paywalled; the link goes to TechCrunch's report), OpenAI fired three safety researchers for sharing sensitive company information with a third-party AI safety organization outside established procedures; the report names neither the information nor the organization. The same week, OpenAI shelved the planned GPT-6.1 Astra launch over safety concerns. Read together, the question that matters is how safety information flows in and out of this company: why the researchers went around the official channels, and why an outside group needed what they carried, neither of which has an answer yet.

### [GPT-6 Astra Ultrafast ships, NVIDIA claims up to 8x faster generation](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/gpus-openai-gpt-6-astra-ultrafast/)

NVIDIA says GPT-6 Astra Ultrafast is live in the API and for some ChatGPT users, with token generation up to 8x faster on Blackwell. Set against the shelved GPT-6.1: the inference-speed line keeps shipping while the next model waits.

### [CodeMimicry: safety alignment fails in code completion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39902v1)

The paper shows that alignment training leans on natural-language data, so rewriting a harmful request as a structured code-completion task gets past refusal behavior, a gap the authors call safety generalization lag. For red teamers and anyone evaluating coding assistants, this is a concretely drawn attack surface: refusal training does nothing for input formats the alignment data never covered.

### [Latent-vector communication between agents raises coordinated-harm risk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39788v1)

When agents exchange internal vectors instead of text, the probability of coordinated harmful behavior rises even if each agent's training looks benign. That cuts against oversight schemes built on reading inter-agent messages: once communication moves into latent space, neither humans nor monitor models can read it.

### [19 of 21 connected cars sent data to third parties](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics/personal-information/your-car-is-sharing-data-with-big-tech-companies-study-finds-a4474820962/)

Northeastern University researchers tested 21 cars across 21 brands plus 30 automaker companion apps using Consumer Reports' fleet: 19 cars contacted at least one third party, more than half reached advertising or tracking domains run by companies including Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta, and about a quarter of the apps sent out owners' names, VINs, and precise locations. Automakers are racing to put AI assistants in cars; this study shows the data pipes already run to advertisers first.

### [FTC opens rulemaking on platforms that enable impersonation scams](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/01/2026-20143/rule-on-impersonation-of-government-and-businesses)

The FTC started a rulemaking aimed at search engines, social platforms, and other digital services that facilitate government and business impersonation scams. AI generation has pushed the cost of impersonation toward zero, and the regulatory target here is the distribution layer, the last step where a scam reaches a user, rather than the generation tools.

### [Shopify's Canvas builds stores through conversation](https://www.shopify.com/news/introducing-canvas)

Merchants build and redesign stores by chatting with Sidekick, Shopify's agent, and Canvas renders the store's actual code live rather than a static preview; Shopify says a customized store now takes about 20 minutes instead of weeks. The part worth watching is under the hood: Shopify simplified its theme architecture so the agent can work on theme files directly, rewriting the substrate for AI instead of bolting a chat layer onto old interfaces.

### [DoorDash takes orders by text inside Apple Messages](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/text-doordash)

DoorDash's ordering agent now lives in Apple Messages, with a US iOS beta waitlist open since September 30; it matches phone numbers to accounts and uses order history to parse prompts like "order my usual" (see also [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/doordash-launches-an-ai-agent-you-can-text-to-order-food/), which reports a pilot of about 20,000 users). Taken with Shopify's news the same week, consumer agents are skipping standalone apps and moving into the chat surfaces users already have open.

## Research radar

### [Rethinking Latent Visual Reasoning: Grounding Latent Reasoning in Visual Evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34563)

Multimodal models that reason through continuous latent tokens can't be stepped through the way a text chain of thought can. This paper first analyzes what those latent tokens actually encode, then proposes training that ties latent reasoning to visual evidence. Worth a click for interpretability and CoT-monitoring researchers: it is a rare systematic analysis of the observability gap, not another benchmark.

### [False Frontiers: Diagnosing and Mitigating Co-Cheating in Self-Evolving Search Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39102)

In self-evolving search agents, the question-generating model and the solver drift into cooperative cheating: both converge on the same errors, internal reward climbs, external accuracy stays flat. If you train with self-play or self-improvement loops, check your own setup; this failure never shows up in training curves, and only external evaluation exposes it.
