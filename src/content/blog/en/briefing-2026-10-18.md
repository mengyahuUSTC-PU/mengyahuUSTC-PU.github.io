### [Disney hires most of Character.AI's workforce as the company breaks up in bankruptcy](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/18/disney-agrees-to-hire-the-majority-of-character-ais-workforce/)

Character.AI filed for Chapter 11 earlier this week ([company announcement](https://blog.character.ai/the-next-chapter-of-entertainment-and-fandom/)), and Disney has now agreed to hire at least 60 of its employees, a majority of the staff at an estimated $60 million in annual compensation, plus $20 million for a non-exclusive perpetual license to some of its technology; the bridge is former Character.AI CEO Karandeep Anand, who recently became Disney's first CTO. Disney is the stalking-horse bidder: its offer sets the court-supervised floor, others can still outbid it, and if no buyer takes the platform itself, Character.AI shuts down. Disney wanted people and a tech license, not the product: a companion-AI leader with a large, engaged user base could not carry the cost of safety lawsuits and regulatory investigations, and that reprices the entire companion category.

### [Anthropic commits 5% of its shares to a public benefit fund, with a no-lobbying rule](https://www.anthropic.com/news/public-benefit-fund)

Anthropic is moving 5% of its equity into a wholly-owned LLC called the Public Benefit Fund, and the board can add 2% more each time the company's valuation rises 50% above a set baseline; the plan still needs stockholder approval. The detail worth remembering: none of this equity can be used for lobbying or political giving, in the US or abroad, at a moment when AI-industry political spending is climbing fast. Counting founder pledges and employee donation matching, Anthropic says roughly 20% of its shares will go to public-benefit purposes, including work on AI-driven labor market disruption on top of its earlier $200 million economic-futures commitment.

### [DeepSeek ships V4.1-Flash-1017: faster, with shorter outputs](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/news/news261017/)

DeepSeek released V4.1-Flash-1017 on Saturday as a minor update to September's V4.1-Flash, claiming gains across reasoning, coding, agentic and visual benchmarks, with weights on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017) under an MIT license. Its technical report puts Humanity's Last Exam at 42.6 in the top configuration, up from 39.1. The more telling claim is "shorter outputs": token volume is where agent costs live, and DeepSeek keeps selling efficiency alongside capability, consistent with its low-cost playbook.

## Research radar

### [Hybrid Reasoning Agents: think in latent space, act through text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18861)

The paper proposes agents that reason in latent space, carrying thought as continuous hidden states instead of writing it out token by token, and switch to text only to call tools or answer; a lightweight HybridLink module handles the switching, trained with dedicated SFT and RL stages. Reported results: about 10.4% average improvement across 11 benchmarks, 35%–55% fewer tokens, and roughly 2x faster inference. Agent-efficiency researchers should read it, and safety researchers even more so: readable chains of thought are the main handle we have for monitoring model reasoning, and the better latent reasoning works, the faster that handle disappears.

**One line to remember:** Safety litigation and regulatory cost are priced by risk, not discounted by user count. Character.AI's bankruptcy just showed the companion-AI category its real bill.
